How to make a Frayer model worksheet

By Cliff Wright · November 3, 2026

A Frayer model is a word map: the vocabulary word sits in the middle, with four boxes around it. You can print one for every word on your list with Live Glossary Pro. It is free for teachers, and you do not need to sign up.

What goes in the four boxes

Each Frayer model on the worksheet has the same four numbered boxes around the word:

  1. Definition. Filled in for you from your list. Students read it and rewrite it in their own words.
  2. Draw a picture. Students sketch something that shows the meaning.
  3. Examples. Lined space for examples of the word.
  4. Non-examples. Lined space for things that are not the word.

Non-examples are what make the Frayer model useful. Deciding what a word is not pushes students to think about where its meaning stops.

Example: a ratios unit

Math words work especially well, because examples and non-examples are easy to write. Copy these four terms into Live Glossary Pro:

ratio — a comparison of two quantities, such as 3 to 4 (3:4)

rate — a ratio that compares two quantities with different units, such as miles per hour

unit rate — a rate in which the second quantity is 1, such as 60 miles per 1 hour

proportion — an equation stating that two ratios are equal

How to make it

Paste your words, one per line, with a dash between each word and its meaning. Choose Frayer, then pick 1 per page for more writing room or 2 per page to save paper. A blank Frayer model is added at the end, so students can map an extra word of their own. The preview shows exactly what prints. Print it, or save it as a PDF.

Quick answers

What is a Frayer model?

A Frayer model is a graphic organizer for one vocabulary word. The word goes in the middle, surrounded by four boxes. In Live Glossary Pro, the boxes are Definition, Draw a picture, Examples and Non-examples.

How many Frayer models fit on a page?

You can choose 1 per page for more writing room or 2 per page to save paper.

How to make a vocabulary quiz

By Cliff Wright · November 3, 2026

Live Glossary Pro turns your vocabulary list into a printable quiz with multiple choice, matching and an answer key. You can print up to four shuffled versions. It is free for teachers, and there is no sign-up.

What is on the quiz

  • Part A, Multiple Choice. Up to 15 questions, each with four choices, A to D (fewer if your list has under four words). The questions alternate between two styles: "What does this word mean?" with meanings as the choices, and "Which word matches this meaning?" with words as the choices.
  • Part B, Matching. Up to 10 words. Students draw a line from each word to its meaning, or write the letter on the blank.

A score box at the top shows the total points, and the answer key is on its own page at the end.

Up to four versions

Choose 1 to 4 versions, labeled A to D. Every version asks the same questions with the same choices, but the question order, the order of the choices and the matching order are all shuffled. Students sitting side by side get different answer letters, and every version is equally hard. Each version gets its own answer key.

Example: a math expressions unit

Copy these six words into Live Glossary Pro and choose Quiz. With six words, students answer six multiple-choice questions and match all six words.

integer — a whole number or its opposite, such as -3, 0 or 5

absolute value — the distance of a number from zero on a number line

inequality — a math sentence that compares two values using <, >, ≤ or ≥

expression — a math phrase with numbers, variables and operations but no equal sign

variable — a letter or symbol that stands for an unknown number

equation — a math sentence that shows two expressions are equal

Quick answers

Can I make different versions of the same vocabulary quiz?

Yes. You can print up to four versions, A to D, with the same questions in a shuffled order and their own answer keys.

How many words do I need for a vocabulary quiz?

At least 2 words. The quiz uses up to 15 words for multiple choice and up to 10 for matching.

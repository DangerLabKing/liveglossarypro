Thanksgiving vocabulary words for grades 3–5

By Cliff Wright · November 3, 2026 · 24 words

Here are 24 Thanksgiving and fall vocabulary words for grades 3–5, each with a kid-friendly meaning. The list covers the harvest and the holiday, the history of the 1621 harvest celebration at Plymouth, including the Wampanoag nation, and a few fall science words. The holiday and the harvest 8 words

Thanksgiving
A holiday in the United States, on the fourth Thursday of November, for giving thanks and sharing a meal.
Gratitude
The feeling of being thankful for what you have.
Harvest
The gathering of ripe crops at the end of the growing season.
Crops
Plants that farmers grow for food or other uses.
Feast
A large, special meal. In 1621, the Plymouth colonists and about 90 Wampanoag men shared a three-day harvest feast.
Tradition
A custom or belief that families or communities pass down over many years.
Autumn
The season between summer and winter, also called fall.
Cornucopia
A horn-shaped basket overflowing with fruits and vegetables, a symbol of a plentiful harvest.

History: Plymouth and the Wampanoag 11 words

Mayflower
The ship that carried 102 passengers from England to North America in 1620.
Voyage
A long trip, especially by sea.
Pilgrims
English passengers on the Mayflower who started a colony in 1620. Many came to worship in their own way.
Colony
A settlement in a new land that is ruled by another country.
Plymouth
The English colony started in 1620 in what is now Massachusetts. It was built where the Wampanoag village of Patuxet had been.
Wampanoag
A Native American nation, the "People of the First Light," who have lived in what is now Massachusetts and Rhode Island for thousands of years and still live there today.
Massasoit
The great leader of the Wampanoag, named Ousamequin, who made a peace agreement with the Plymouth colonists in 1621.
Tisquantum
A Wampanoag man from Patuxet, also called Squanto, who learned English after being kidnapped to Europe. He translated for the colonists and showed them how to plant corn.
Indigenous
Describes the first people to live in a place and their descendants.
Three Sisters
Corn, beans and squash planted together. Native peoples grew them as a team because each plant helps the others grow.
Ancestor
A family member who lived long before you, like a great-great-grandparent.

Fall and Thanksgiving today 5 words

Migration
The movement of animals from one place to another as the seasons change.
Deciduous
Describes trees that lose their leaves every fall.
Proclamation
An official announcement. In 1863, President Abraham Lincoln's proclamation made Thanksgiving a national holiday.
Parade
A line of people, floats and bands that march through the streets to celebrate.
Volunteer
A person who helps others without being paid, like serving meals at a food bank.

About the history words

The history words describe the 1621 harvest celebration at Plymouth as it happened. The Wampanoag appear as a nation with thousands of years of history who still live in Massachusetts and Rhode Island today, and Massasoit and Tisquantum are named as the real people they were. The words pair well with your social studies lessons.
